Roots & Resistance

A Climate Justice Story Rooted in Resistance

Why I Created This Podcast

Roots & Resistance was created from a belief that climate stories should not only be told through statistics and global reports, but also through human experiences, memories, and emotions. This project explores climate justice through a personal lens, connecting my experience growing up in Myanmar with wider conversations about inequality, displacement, and resistance.

The Thinking Behind the Design

Rather than following a traditional information-based podcast format, I chose storytelling as a way to create connection. Inspired by feminist, Indigenous, and decolonial approaches to knowledge, the podcast focuses on relationships, emotions, and lived experiences. The aim was not only to make listeners understand climate injustice, but also to encourage them to feel and reflect.

From Academic Research to Personal Storytelling

Academic ideas such as intersectionality, slow violence, and climate justice shaped the foundation of this project. However, I wanted to translate these concepts into a more accessible story that connects academic knowledge with everyday experiences. This project taught me that communication is not only about delivering information, but also about creating spaces for empathy and dialogue.
